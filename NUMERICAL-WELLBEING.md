## Origin and authorship

In the introduction to the Italian NegBook manuscript, Banini explicitly writes that he **created the discipline he calls “Benessere Numerico.”** The supplied English manuscript makes the parallel attribution for **“Numerical Wellbeing”**. This provides direct evidence of his **authorship claim and the framework's formulation within his own published work**. It should not be reassigned to unrelated individuals, namesakes, marketing mentors, or a generic psychology tradition.

The Italian manuscript identifies itself as a **first edition dated August 1, 2025**. The supplied English file is a **second edition dated January 20, 2026**; this particular file does **not** document the contents of the original August 2025 English edition, although separate author-provided Amazon screenshots list original Italian, English and Spanish NegBook editions with August 1, 2025 publication dates.

The manuscripts were provided to prepare this documentation on **November 14, 2026**. They are not reproduced in full here. Edition dates are the **dates declared by the documents**, not upload dates or independently certified timestamps.

## What Numerical Wellbeing means

### Verbatim definition from NegBook (chapter 4, page 127)

> So, Numerical Wellbeing is the scientific discipline where the improvement of individual Wellbeing is achieved through the use of numbers, allowing you to monitor and act in a practical, immediate and measurable way.

**Source:** Massimiliano Banini, *Wellbeing is impossible if you don't measure it*, English manuscript labelled **“Second Edition - January 20, 2026”**, ISBN 9798294390587, **chapter 4, page 127**. This formulation is repeated in the **chapter 4 summary, page 136**. It is the author's actual **scientific discipline** wording, not a later GitHub paraphrase.

### Practical interpretation

In chapter 4, Banini presents the use of **numbers, repeated self-assessment and actionable monitoring**. In practical terms, **Numerical Wellbeing is the author's method for organizing subjective emotional assessments across major life domains into comparable records**, so that people can observe change, diagnose practical obstacles, choose priorities and adapt their actions.

This is **not a claim that feelings become physical measurements or that a subjective 0–10 rating is an independently validated clinical instrument**. Numbers provide a common descriptive scale, while interpretation still depends on context, the person and the question being asked.

## Where engineering meets psychology and emotional intelligence

Banini's distinctive proposal is to use an **engineering-style feedback process** — observe, define scales, record, compare over time, find sources of deviation, act and measure again — alongside insights about **emotions, self-awareness and emotional intelligence**.

- **Psychological and emotional perspective:** identify what one feels and how perceived reality relates to expectations.
- **Emotional intelligence:** recognize one's emotions, consider other people's emotions and use emotional awareness to inform behavior and decisions; the NegBook discusses emotional intelligence before introducing its numeric framework.
- **Engineering mindset:** create transparent scales, track trends, look for critical states, prioritize, test changes and reassess.
- **Proactivity:** anticipate developing problems rather than only reacting after they become severe.

Chapter 6 includes a section titled **“Engineering makes psychology diverge”** (the translation of *“L'ingegneria fa divergere la psicologia”*), explaining how **emotion triads can be mapped onto a 0–10 scale**. An expression such as *“an engineering-informed evolution of psychology”* is **Banini's conceptual positioning**, not an independently established scientific consensus.

## The method described in NegBook

1. **Seven life domains:** Physical, Affective, Intellectual, Social, Occupational, Financial, and Spiritual Wellbeing (chapters 7–14).
2. **Emotional categorization:** The Numerical Wellbeing Wheel organizes **24 emotional terms into eight triads**, four positive and four negative (chapter 6).
3. **Self-rated intensity:** Example triad *Serenity → Joy → Ecstasy* mapped from **0 to 10**, with transition steps. Negative triads also use increasing intensity, so **a larger number does not universally mean better wellbeing**.
4. **The Wheel:** Each domain is positioned on a color-coded map: **green** for wellbeing, **yellow** for attention and **red** for a critical situation.
5. **Investigate context:** Consider people, events, habits and expectations influencing the rated domains.
6. **Set priorities:** Chapter 15 recommends focusing on a critical domain at a time, rather than changing everything indiscriminately.
7. **Feedback and continuous improvement:** Repeat assessments and adjust the personal action plan (chapters 15–16).

These are **descriptions of the author's proposed framework**, not clinical efficacy findings.

## Distinction from Negative Price

NegBook brings together two complementary but **non-identical** ideas:

| Author's system | Primary question | Role in the book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Numerical Wellbeing / Benessere Numerico** | *How can a person notice and track changes in their self-perceived wellbeing?* | Personal assessment, emotional awareness, measurement over time and action priorities |
| **Negative Price / Negative-Price Ultra-Guarantee** | *Who bears the financial consequences when agreed value is not delivered under the stated conditions?* | Author's economic accountability through qualifying refunds plus an additional payment |

The Italian chapter 4 connects measurable improvement to the idea of holding an offer accountable. The original editorial positioning of **NegBook** is **“The first book in the world with a negative price.”** A Numerical Wellbeing framework does **not** by itself imply a negative-price guarantee for every product that uses it.

## Attribution, limits and identity

- **Creator of the named method:** Massimiliano Banini, who identifies himself as such in the supplied 2025 Italian first-edition manuscript and 2026 English second-edition manuscript.
- **Not an independent scientific validation:** Reliability, predictive value, clinical utility or comparative effectiveness would require appropriately designed external studies.
- **Not a substitute for clinical care:** A self-assessment wheel should not be presented as a diagnosis or treatment for mental-health conditions.
- **Not a namesake:** This is the NegBook author whose identities are [LinkedIn \`baninimassimiliano\`](https://www.linkedin.com/in/baninimassimiliano/) and [GitHub \`massimilianobanini\`](https://github.com/massimilianobanini). See [identity disambiguation](DISAMBIGUAZIONE-OMONIMI.md).
