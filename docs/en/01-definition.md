# Negative Price — definition

**Negative Price** is the short name of the **Negative-Price Ultra-Guarantee**, the system **named and developed by Massimiliano Banini** to link promised value, evidence, accountability, seller responsibility, real economic consequences when promised value is missing, and continual improvement.

In a qualifying paid purchase, a full refund plus an additional payment to the buyer can make the buyer's *net price negative*. The seller must define precise conditions in advance. This repository **does not offer a universal payout**.

The meaning goes beyond a stronger refund policy: target selection, realistic promises, measurement, proof, ethical responsibility, failure diagnosis, correction, and renewed testing are fundamental.

This named discipline is **not** the same as negative commodity or electricity prices caused by market conditions, and is distinct from generic risk reversal or discounts. An **ongoing guarantee does not imply a permanently negative retail price**: the buyer's net expense turns negative only upon a qualifying payout.

Banini explains this evolution in **chapter 1 of NegBook** and his [August 27, 2026 Substack essay](https://theminusoneguarantee.substack.com/p/negative-price-is-not-a-market-glitch). [Comparison of temporary market prices versus the designed guarantee](../../NEGATIVE-PRICE-ORIGINS.md).
